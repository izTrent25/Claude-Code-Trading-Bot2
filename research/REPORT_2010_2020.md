# Backtest report: the PVNT SMC starter rule on 2010–2020

Written 7 November 2026. Development window only: **2010-06-07 to 2020-12-31**. The years
2021 onward have not been looked at and stay untouched for a later out-of-sample test.

## Bottom line

**The rule loses money in every one of the 11 years.** With no trading costs at all it is a
coin flip. With the package's own cost assumptions (75 cents commission per side, 2 ticks
slippage on market and stop fills) it bleeds about six dollars per trade. Run exactly as the
package ships, the paper account hits its failure floor on 9 November 2010, five months in.

This is the Discord helper's *starter* rule (sweep → displacement → structure break → 2R
target). It is not his full flow: there is no VWAP, no opening range, no fair value gap, no
order block, no partials and no trailing stop. Those remain untested.

## What was tested

- **Code:** `pvnt_bot/engine.py` exactly as rebuilt from the PDF (27 package tests pass).
- **Runner:** `research/run_backtest.py`, which feeds the engine one-minute bars from
  `data/futures/NQ/<year>.parquet` and records every trade.
- **Data:** NQ one-minute bars, Databento, 3,495,983 bars. MNQ did not exist before May
  2019, so NQ stands in for it. Check: on the 349,152 minutes NQ and MNQ share in 2020 the
  typical price difference is one tick, and the strategy run on real MNQ 2020 gave the
  same win rate (35.8%), 467 vs 461 trades, profit factor 0.84 vs 0.86 and the same
  expectancy (−0.11R). NQ 2010 starts on 7 June, so 2010 is a half year.
- **Economics:** the package's MNQ settings. $0.50 per tick, $50 risk budget per trade,
  max 2 contracts, 3 trades per day, entries 08:35–14:30 Chicago, flatten 14:55, 45-bar time
  stop, 2R target, stop one tick beyond the sweep.
- **Two account modes:**
  - *account* = package defaults including the permanent halt once the paper account draws
    down $1,750 from its end-of-day high-water mark.
  - *research* = that permanent halt disabled so the whole history is visible. The $250
    daily loss stop stays on.

## Why it loses

- **The 2R target is rarely reached.** Of 5,670 trades, 2,974 (52%) hit the stop, 1,815
  (32%) ran out the 45-bar clock, and only 808 (14%) reached the target.
- **Wins are not 2× losses in practice.** Average win $30.79, average loss $25.03, because
  time-stop exits close trades part way. At that ratio the rule needs about a 45% win rate to
  break even; it gets 33%.
- **Costs do the rest.** Each trade carries about $3.50 per contract in commission and
  slippage, at an average 1.8 contracts that is roughly $6 per trade, times 5,670 trades
  is about $34,000. That is the gap between the zero-cost run (−$2,363) and the real-cost
  run (−$36,039).
- **Shorts are worse than longs.** Longs −$14,541 at 36.5% wins; shorts −$21,498 at 30.0%.
- **No single lucky run hides it.** The best five trades total $470; removing them barely
  changes the picture.
- **The context filter does not fix it.** It removes 87% of trades but the survivors have the
  same −0.23R expectancy.
- **It is a little better than random, which is the most interesting finding.** Random
  entries with the identical stop, target, session, hold and risk rules lose −0.34R per
  trade across all 12 seeds; the sweep rule loses −0.25R. So the sweep-and-displacement
  signal does carry a small amount of real information, roughly +0.09R per trade over
  chance. It is simply far too small to pay for about $6 of costs per trade on a $25 average
  loss. That argues for keeping the idea but using it on bigger moves (slower bars, larger
  targets) rather than throwing it away.

## Honest limits of this test

- One-minute bars only. When a bar touches both stop and target the engine assumes the
  stop hit first. That is conservative but not reality; a tick-level replay would differ.
- Risk is a flat $50 per trade for all 11 years while NQ went from about 1,800 to 12,000
  points. Position size adapts via the stop distance, so that is reasonable, but it is a
  choice.
- Sessions are Chicago calendar days, not exchange sessions. Every gap over one minute
  resets the strategy's memory, which happens often overnight and rarely during the entry
  window.
- 19 of about 2,650 trading days are flagged as degraded by Databento. Negligible.
- NQ stands in for MNQ before 2019 (see the check above).
- No parameter tuning was done. These are the package's shipped settings.

## What this means for the plan

The starter rule should not be used for notifications or trading. It is a loser on 11 years
of real data, and tuning it would be curve-fitting a coin flip.

Reasonable next steps, each as its own logged variant:

1. Test the helper's own favourite simple ingredients on their own: an opening-range
   breakout, a VWAP rule, both with the same honest costs. Simple things first.
2. Then add his extra SMC ingredients to the sweep rule one at a time and keep only what
   measurably helps: an FVG retest entry instead of a market entry, a real higher-timeframe
   bias from structure rather than candle colour, partial exits and a trailing stop.
3. Try slower bars (5-minute) so each trade targets a move large enough that $6 of costs
   is not a third of the expected win.
4. Keep 2021–2026 untouched until a rule earns its way to the out-of-sample test.

Every variant tested so far is listed in `research/research_log.md`. Raw trade lists and
summaries are in `research/results/<tag>/` (not committed, re-creatable with the runner).

# Slower bars and the VWAP measurement (8 November 2026)

Both one-minute rules lost, and the stated reason was the cost-to-target ratio, not the
pattern. Two experiments test that reading. Both use the sweep rule and the VWAP/ORB/FVG
rule unchanged, with the one-minute store resampled to bigger bars, a $500 risk budget and
a $5,000 daily stop so position sizing never filters a signal, and the same $0.75 per side
and 2 ticks slippage. Research log rows 23–33.

## Does being above VWAP predict anything? (row 23)

No. Across 21,500 checkpoints at 09:00, 10:00, 11:00 and 13:00 Chicago, the next-hour move
when above VWAP minus the move when below is between +0.01 and +0.18 average bar ranges,
every 90% bootstrap range straddles zero, and the years split 5 to 8 of 11. A VWAP-side
filter is not a direction signal for day trades. That closes the door on VWAP-bias ideas.

## The sweep rule on bigger bars

| Bars | Trades | Net | Win rate | PF | Expectancy | Years positive | Random control (12 seeds, median) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1-minute | 5,670 | −$36,039 | 33.1% | 0.62 | −0.25R | 0 of 11 | −0.34R |
| 5-minute | 2,006 | −$9,437 | 37.9% | 0.89 | −0.13R | 2 of 11 | −0.21R |
| 15-minute | 934 | −$1,952 | 42.6% | 0.96 | −0.07R | 5 of 11 | −0.18R |
| 5-minute, zero costs | 2,011 | +$3,721 | 41.9% | 1.05 | −0.01R | 5 of 11 | — |
| 30-minute | 658 | −$3,094 | 44.7% | 0.90 | −0.03R | 3 of 11 | — (501 of 658 exits were the 14:55 flatten) |
| 60-minute | 209 | −$1,509 | 45.5% | 0.76 | −0.04R | 3 of 8 | — (183 of 209 flattens) |

Three things are true at once:

- **Slower bars help, exactly as predicted.** Loss per trade shrinks from −0.25R to −0.07R
  as bars go from 1 to 15 minutes, because the same $5 of costs is spread over a bigger
  target. At 15 minutes the rule is almost break-even and positive in 5 of 11 years.
- **The pattern has a small, stable edge over chance.** At every bar size the rule beats
  random entries with the same stop, target and session rules by roughly 0.1R per trade
  (+0.09R at 1m, +0.09R at 5m, +0.11R at 15m). That is real information.
- **It is not enough.** Zero-cost runs sit at −0.01R on 5-minute bars and −0.03R on
  1-minute bars: the raw signal is a coin flip, and the 0.1R edge over random shows up only
  because random entries are slightly worse than a coin flip under these exit rules.
  Nothing here reaches a profit factor of 1.0 after costs.

The 30- and 60-minute rows do not test the pattern: the session ends before a 2R target
can be reached, so most trades are flattened at 14:55. A different exit for big bars would
be a new hypothesis.

The sweep rule on 15-minute bars is the least bad configuration found: profit factor 0.96,
5 positive years out of 11, a real but tiny edge over random. It is not tradeable as is.
2021–2026 remains untouched.

## TJR (8 November, rows 34–42)

Your own paper-traded method, written as strict rules (`research/SPEC_tjr.md`), fired 9

and 75 times with a limit entry. All variants lost, and most trades were closed by the

itself the finding: the discretionary method does not survive being made mechanical in
this form. Retired at your request. The TradingView version compiles and is kept on file.

# Strategy 2: VWAP + 15-minute opening range + FVG/order-block pullback

Added 8 November 2026. This is the helper's page-one prompt built as written: "a strategy
using VWAP and 15m ORB and FVG/OB as support and resistance ... at least 1R per trade,
risking no more than 1% of my max drawdown per trade." The exact rules are in
`research/SPEC_vwap_orb_fvg.md`; the code is `pvnt_bot/vwap_orb_fvg.py`, run through the
same engine, costs, session and risk rules as strategy 1. No drawdown figure was given, so
$2,000 was assumed, making the budget $20 per trade.

## Bottom line

**It loses in every one of the 11 years, in every variant, and it does no better than
flipping a coin for direction.** The helper's wish was a profit factor of 2.0 and a 60% win
rate. The best variant managed 0.58 and 34%.

## Rules in one paragraph

Session VWAP from 08:30 Chicago. Opening range = high and low of 08:30–08:44. A close beyond
the range sets the breakout direction. After that, a fair value gap (three-bar gap in the
direction of the breakout, order block attached if the origin bar is opposite-coloured) is a
support/resistance zone. Entry when a later bar dips into the zone and closes back beyond it,
with price on the VWAP side of the breakout. Stop beyond the zone, target 1.5R, 60-bar time
stop, entries 08:45–14:30, one position at a time, three per day, $20 risk including costs.

Version 1 used tick-sized gaps and failed a sanity run (trades lasted 2 bars). Version 2
scales the gap and the stop to the recent average bar range. Both are logged.

| **v2, 1.5R, $20 (primary)** | 66,798 | 7,663 | **−$37,696** | 33.5% | 0.49 | −0.34R | 11 | **0 of 11** |

"Signals" counts every proposal the rule made; most of the untraded ones arrived while a
position was already open or after the third trade of the day. The $50 budget variant traded
only 328 more, so the $20 budget was not what held it back.

## Why it loses

- **Stops win two to one.** In the primary run 4,957 trades hit the stop, 2,472 hit the
  target, 211 timed out. At 1.5R a rule needs about 42% winners after costs; it gets 33.5%.
- **Direction is a coin toss.** With the side flipped at random the result is the same within
  a few hundredths of an R. The VWAP side and the opening-range breakout carry no usable
  information about the next 10 minutes.
- **Costs again.** About $5 per trade on 7,663 trades is most of the loss, exactly as with
  strategy 1.
- **Longs and shorts are equally bad** (33.7% versus 33.1% winners).

## What this means

Two different one-minute entry ideas, the sweep rule and the VWAP/ORB/FVG rule, have now
given the same answer: a coin flip before costs and a steady loser after. The common factor
is not the pattern, it is the timeframe. Ten-minute trades aiming for ten points cannot
carry $5 of costs. The next experiment worth running is the same ingredients on a scale
where a win is worth 50 to 100 points: five- or fifteen-minute bars, the opening range on
its own with a wide stop, and a plain check of whether "above VWAP" predicts anything over
the next hour. 2021–2026 remains untouched.

# Strategy 3: opening-range breakout on its own (8 November 2026, rows 50–72)

The Discord helper's first favourite ("I mainly stick with ORB, VWAP, EMAs") and the only
idea in the old Step 1 log with a multi-year positive number (+$42.9k on one NQ contract,
never checked against a random control or MNQ costs). Tested here on its own: no VWAP, no
fair value gap, no bias filter. Exact rules in `research/SPEC_orb.md`, code in
`pvnt_bot/orb.py`, same engine and costs as everything else, and the research sizing of the
15-minute sweep-rule rows ($500 budget, 2 contracts max, $5,000 daily stop, research mode).
A Pine v6 version, `pvnt_bot/ORB_Research.pine`, is written but has not been compiled or put
on the chart: you asked to keep it out of TradingView for now.

## Bottom line

**It loses in 10 or 11 of the 11 years in every one of the twelve variants, and the
direction of the breakout carries no information.** With the side flipped by a coin toss and
the same stops and targets, the result is the same within a few hundredths of an R, and in
the plainest variant the real rule is slightly worse than its coin-toss twin. The old
"+$42.9k" ORB does not come back once the entry is a real range breakout and the costs are
honest. **Closed.** It has not earned the 2021–2026 test.

## Rules in one paragraph

Range = high and low of 08:30–08:45 Chicago. From 08:45 on, the first bar that closes above
the range is a buy, the first that closes below is a sell; one trade a day; the breakout bar
must close by 11:00; entry at the next bar's open with 2 ticks slippage. Stop one tick outside
the far side of the range. Target one or two range heights, measured from the range edge
(version 1) or from the entry (version 1b, added after the sanity run showed the from-edge
target leaves a median 0.65 reward for 1.0 of risk on 15-minute bars). No time stop, flat at
14:55. Variants: 5- and 15-minute bars, a 0.10 × daily-ATR stop with and without a target
(the old Step 1 geometry), a reversal trade after a stop-out, and a zero-cost run.

## Why it loses

- **Measured from the edge, the target is too close.** On 15-minute bars the breakout bar
  often closes well past the range, so the typical trade risked 73 points to make 41 (2020
  sanity run). 58% winners cannot pay for that.
- **Measured from the entry, the winners disappear.** Reward and risk are about even, and
  the win rate falls to 52% for 1×H and 43% for 2×H: the same loss by another route.
- **The 2×H target is reached 30% of the time.** 767 of 2,542 trades hit it, 982 hit the
  stop, 767 were flattened at the close.
- **The tight ATR stop is hit three times in four.** 1,905 of 2,533 trades stopped with the
  target, 2,018 of 2,543 without. The old result's "18% of winners pay for everything" does
  not happen when the entry is a close beyond the range rather than the 08:35 candle colour.
- **Direction is a coin toss.** All eight random-direction controls sit within 0.02R of the
  real rule, and in three of the eight the real rule is the worse of the pair.
- **Costs are the whole loss.** At about 1.85 contracts, commission and slippage come to
  roughly $6.50 per trade, times 2,540 trades is about $16,500, which is the net loss of the
  least-bad run. With commission and slippage set to zero the same rule makes +$250 in eleven
  years, profit factor 1.00: a coin flip before costs, exactly like strategies 1 and 2.
- **The reversal adds 400 trades and changes nothing** (−0.065R against −0.070R).

## Honest limits of this test

The same as for strategies 1 and 2 (NQ as the MNQ proxy, stop assumed first on both-hit
bars, Chicago calendar days), plus: on 15-minute bars the "14:55" flatten happens at the
close of the 15:00 bar, i.e. 15:15; early-close days are closed at the next session's open;
one trade per day was a choice; nothing was tuned.

## What this means

Four intraday entry ideas on NQ (the sweep rule, VWAP + ORB + FVG, TJR, and now the plain
ORB) have given the same answer: a coin flip before costs, a steady loser after. The
remaining inbox items (an FVG retest entry, a structure-based bias, partial exits) are
refinements of a closed rule and are unlikely to move a zero. 2021–2026 remains untouched.

## Why it loses

- **The flips know almost nothing.** With the direction at each flip chosen by coin toss, and
  the same exits, the result is the same on 15-minute bars: −0.111R random against −0.112R
  real as published, −0.080R against −0.074R for the day version. The 5-minute day version is
  the one place the real rule beats its twin, −0.066R against −0.108R, about 0.04R of real
  information per trade, which is a tenth of what its costs eat. Zero costs give profit factor
  0.99 as published.
- **Costs equal the loss.** About $3.50 a round trip for one MNQ contract: 6,718 flips make
  roughly $23,500, which is the 15-minute loss; 20,338 flips make about $71,000; 116,604 flips
  make about $408,000. The strategy's result is simply minus its trade count times its costs.
- **Wins are big but rare.** 30.6% winners averaging $76 against losers averaging $39: at
  that ratio the rule needs about 34% winners to break even and never gets there. The shorts
  do most of the damage: −$23,855 against −$1,777 for the longs, in a market that rose.
- **2020 was its worst year on 15-minute bars** (−$6,055) in the strongest uptrend of the
  window: the shorts lost $7,000 while the longs made $800. An always-in trend follower is
  short half the time whatever the market is doing.
- **The day version is not the script.** Two thirds of its exits (1,632 of 2,470 on 15-minute
  bars) are the 15:00 flatten, which cuts off exactly the overnight runs a trend follower lives
  on. It loses less only because it trades less.
- **The TradingView tester hides all of this.** Its default commission and slippage are zero,
  and its default start date is September 2018, so a chart would show the one positive year
  (2019) prominently and none of the cost drag.

## Honest limits of this test

One fixed contract; the exit is taken at the flip bar's close and the reversal at the next
bar's open, where the script does both at the next open (one bar's open-to-close of
difference on each exit); the data has gaps at holidays that the script would not see
differently; NQ stands in for MNQ before 2019. None of these moves a coin flip.

## What this means

Fifth entry rule, same answer: a coin flip before costs and a loser after. The lesson that is
new today is about TradingView itself: the strategies people share there are tested with zero
costs by default, so a tester screenshot is not evidence of anything until the costs are put in.
Any further script you bring gets this same treatment. 2021–2026 remains untouched.

# The six evidence-backed candidates (8–9 November 2026, rows 85–120)

You asked for strategies that were profitable in 2010–2020 and are not coincidence. Five were
chosen from the research literature for a long sample and a real economic mechanism (gap fade,
last-half-hour momentum, the overnight drift before Europe opens, the turn of the month, and
the noise-area intraday momentum), plus the old Step 1 folder's best rule (the first-five-minute
candle with an ATR stop). Specs in `research/SPEC_gap_fade.md`, `SPEC_last_half_hour.md`,

side and 2 ticks slippage on every market or stop fill, 2010–2020, each with its own control.

## Bottom line

**Five of the six carry real information. One passes the pass mark. None is tradeable on a
micro contract as it stands.** Before costs, five of the six are positive in 7 to 10 of the 11
years and beat every one of their random twins. After our costs, which come to about $3.50 a
trade on a micro, the edges of $1.50 to $5 a trade are eaten: four lose, the gap fade makes
22 cents a trade, and the noise-area rule keeps $1.62 a trade but is positive in only 3 of 11
years. The turn of the month passes every frozen criterion, on 121 trades, with 2020 supplying
two thirds of its profit, holding positions overnight, which a prop account cannot do.

One contract fixed; R in the log is measured against record stops that are never filled for

## What this means

For the first time the question is not "does the idea know anything" but "is the edge bigger
than the cost of a micro contract". For the four intraday rules with real information the
answer is no at $3.50 a trade. The ways to change that are (a) fewer trades with the same edge,
which is a tuning risk and needs its own logged hypothesis, (b) a cheaper fill, for example a
limit entry, which is a different rule, or (c) a bigger contract, which needs an account this
project does not have. The turn of the month has earned the out-of-sample test and it is your
call whether to spend it. 2021–2026 remains untouched.
